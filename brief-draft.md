# Nashville's curb-ramp audits: 1 in 2 audited ramp records is recorded as conforming, and 961 records describe a location with no ramp

**One-line summary:** Of the 18,388 records in Nashville's Curb Ramps layer, 9,103 (49.5 percent) are recorded Yes for conformance, 4,035 (21.9 percent) are recorded No, 2,425 (13.2 percent) Maximum Extent Feasible, and 1,226 (6.7 percent) Technically Infeasible, with 961 (5.2 percent) recorded No Ramp, 470 (2.6 percent) Unknown, and 168 (0.9 percent) blank; District 18 records the lowest Yes share at 36.3 percent (273 of 752) and District 11 records both a 39.8 percent Yes share and the highest No Ramp share, 134 of its 704 records (19.0 percent).

Start with the scale. The layer holds 18,388 audited curb-ramp records across all 35 Metro Council districts. Machine Readable pulled the full layer from Metro's ArcGIS service on November 11, 2026; the pulled row count matches the server's own count for the layer exactly (18,388). One row is one audited ramp record, identified by its on-street, from-street, and side. The layer records locations that were audited; it contains no record for a corner that was never audited, so nothing in this brief measures how many corners in Nashville have never been looked at. Audits were recorded between July 9, 1998 and May 6, 2025 (America/Chicago local dates), and 1,338 records (7.3 percent) also carry a re-audit date, recorded between July 4, 2022 and May 27, 2024.

## Conformance, as recorded

- As recorded and normalized (see Methods for the normalization rule), ConformID reads: Yes 9,103 (49.5 percent of all 18,388 records), No 4,035 (21.9 percent), Maximum Extent Feasible 2,425 (13.2 percent), Technically Infeasible 1,226 (6.7 percent), No Ramp 961 (5.2 percent), Unknown 470 (2.6 percent), and Blank 168 (0.9 percent). Human scale (derived): about 1 in 2 audited ramp records is recorded Yes.
- On the narrower base of the 16,789 records carrying one of the four determined conformance categories (Yes, No, Maximum Extent Feasible, Technically Infeasible), the Yes share is 54.2 percent. The two bases are printed separately throughout this brief and are not interchangeable; the headline figures use the full 18,388-record denominator.
- The raw ConformID field holds 13 distinct values. Alongside YES (8,568), NO (3,212), MAX_EXT_FEASIBLE (1,864), TECH_INFEASIBLE (1,005), and NO RAMP (867), the field carries case and spelling variants of the same categories (Yes 535, No 823, Maximum Extent Feasible 561, Technically Infeasable 221, No Ramp 94), two Unknown spellings (353 and 117), and 168 blanks. The normalization merges only those variants; the full raw counter is in data/conform_raw_panel.csv. The layer metadata does not define any of the categories, so this brief reports each category under its recorded name and assigns no definition the metadata does not carry.

## Where the Yes share is lowest, and where there is no ramp

District figures use the 17,524 records whose CouncilDistrict field names a district from 1 to 35; 861 records name no district and 3 records carry district 0, and those 864 records are excluded from every district figure below (see Methods).

- By Yes share, District 18 records the lowest share: 273 of its 752 records (36.3 percent) are Yes, and 209 (27.8 percent) are No. District 11 records 280 Yes of 704 (39.8 percent), District 21 records 574 Yes of 1,376 (41.7 percent), and District 5 records 385 Yes of 904 (42.6 percent).
- By No count, District 19 holds the most records recorded No, 610 of its 2,212 records (27.6 percent); District 19 is also the largest district in the layer by record count, and its Yes share is 52.2 percent (1,154 of 2,212). District 21 holds the second-largest No count, 320.
- No Ramp records concentrate in District 11: 134 of its 704 records (19.0 percent) are recorded No Ramp, against 961 of 18,388 (5.2 percent) layer-wide. District 35 records the second-highest No Ramp share, 93 of 539 (17.3 percent). These are shares of audited records in each district, not shares of corners: the layer holds no count of unaudited corners.

## The No Ramp records are a different kind of record

- The 961 No Ramp records differ from the rest of the layer on every administrative field. 876 of them (91.2 percent) carry ProjectType UNKNOWN, against 924 ProjectType UNKNOWN records in the whole layer. 618 of them (64.3 percent) carry no audit date at all, against 724 undated records layer-wide, and only 343 carry an audit date. 619 of them (64.4 percent) carry a blank DetectableWarning value, and only 7 carry the value 1.
- Because of that profile, this brief prints No Ramp as its own category throughout and never merges it into No: a No record describes an audited ramp recorded as not conforming, while a No Ramp record describes a location recorded as having no ramp, most of them outside the dated audit waves.

## Audit era is not construction era

- By the era in which the audit was recorded, the Yes share reads: audited before 2010, 63.5 percent (3,048 of 4,802); audited 2010-2019, 54.4 percent (1,460 of 2,684); audited 2020-2026, 45.1 percent (4,592 of 10,178). The 724 records with no audit date record a Yes share of 0.4 percent (3 records), because 618 of them are No Ramp records.
- DateAudited is the date the audit was recorded, not the date a ramp was built, and the layer carries no construction date. The era figures therefore cannot be read as a trend in ramp construction. They are also not geographically uniform: a single audit year, 2023, holds 7,899 records (43.0 percent of the layer), and that wave is concentrated in a few districts - District 19 holds 2,043 of the 2023 records, District 21 holds 1,220, District 17 holds 866, and District 6 holds 778, while 853 of the 2023 records carry no district. An era comparison on this layer compares different audit waves over different parts of the city, and this brief prints it on that basis only.
- Re-audits are a small part of the record: 1,338 records carry a DateReAudited, and among them 642 (48.0 percent) are recorded Yes and 420 (31.4 percent) No - a distribution close to the layer as a whole, printed here as a description of the re-audited group, not as a before-and-after measure, because the layer records one conformance value per record, not a sequence.

## What the No records say, and the detectable-warning field

- ConformExplain is a free-text field with 2,855 distinct values; 10,479 records carry a blank, single-space, or punctuation-only value. Among the 4,035 records recorded No, the most common explanations are "Landing Exceeds 2%" (544 records), "Ramp To Street Exceeds 8.3% And Is <8Ft" (259), a no-detectable-warning explanation in two spellings (207 and 43), "Landing > 2%" (154), "Landing Is <5Ft" (126), and "Flush Mount Is >1/4"" (87). The explanation field is not a coded category, so these counts describe the most common recorded strings, not an exhaustive classification of the No records.
- DetectableWarning is recorded as 1 on 8,056 records, 0 on 9,702, and blank on 630. On the 17,758 records carrying a coded value (0 or 1), the value-1 share is 45.4 percent. Among coded Yes records the value-1 share is 50.9 percent (4,632 of 9,100), and among coded No records it is 36.8 percent (1,485 of 4,034). The layer metadata does not define the 0/1 coding, so this brief reports the recorded value and its shares, on the coded base only, and does not name what the value represents beyond the field's name.

## The recorded category agrees with the recorded measurements

Two internal controls, described in verification.md, check the conformance category against the slope measurements recorded on the same records, using the two thresholds printed in the layer's own explanation strings (a landing over 2 percent, and a ramp slope over 8.3 percent):

- Landing: on records recorded No, the largest of the four recorded landing slopes exceeds 2 percent on 1,705 of 3,993 records with a landing measurement (42.7 percent); on records recorded Yes, it exceeds 2 percent on 629 of 9,077 (6.9 percent).
- Ramp slope to the street: on records recorded No, the larger of the two recorded perpendicular slopes exceeds 8.3 percent on 1,090 of 3,808 records with a perpendicular measurement (28.6 percent); on records recorded Yes, on 291 of 8,706 (3.3 percent).

The category and the measurements therefore move together in the direction the explanation strings describe. Individual slope fields also carry a small number of extreme recorded values (for example a LeftSlope maximum of 307.0, on 7 records over 30 across that field); no figure in this brief rests on those tails, because every slope figure here is a share of records over a threshold, not an average.

## Methods

Population: all 18,388 records in the Curb_Ramps_view layer (ArcGIS FeatureServer layer 0, services2.arcgis.com/HdTo6HJqh92wn4D8), pulled November 11, 2026 with 1,000-record pagination ordered by OBJECTID; the pulled count was verified against the server's returnCountOnly count (18,388) at pull time. The service listing shows the layer's data was last edited November 10, 2026. OBJECTID is the row key: CurbRampID is not unique (18,156 distinct values on 18,388 records). Conformance normalization: the recorded ConformID value is trimmed and case-folded; YES variants merge to Yes, NO variants to No, MAX_EXT_FEASIBLE and its spelled-out variant merge to Maximum Extent Feasible, TECH_INFEASIBLE and its misspelled variant (Technically Infeasable) merge to Technically Infeasible, NO RAMP variants merge to No Ramp, and Unknown spellings merge to Unknown; blank values (168 records) are carried as Blank on the full 18,388-record denominator in every headline share. District figures use only the 17,524 records naming exactly one district number from 1 to 35; 861 records name none and 3 carry district 0. Audit eras group the America/Chicago local date of DateAudited; 724 records carry no audit date and form their own group. DetectableWarning shares use only the 17,758 records carrying 0 or 1; blank values (630) are excluded from that base and the exclusion is printed wherever a DetectableWarning figure appears. Slope figures use the maximum of the recorded slope fields named in each control, on records carrying at least one of those measurements; recorded 0 values are measurements (a flat slope), not missing values, and are kept. Dates are epoch-millisecond values in the service, converted to America/Chicago local dates. No join to any other dataset was performed. Category stability: the layer is an accumulation of audit waves from 1998 to 2025, not a current-condition snapshot with a time series; the 2023 wave alone is 43.0 percent of the layer and is geographically concentrated, so no trend over time is computed and audit-era figures are printed with that warning. Exposure: the layer contains only audited locations; it contains no count of unaudited corners and no walking-volume measure, so no completeness rate, no per-corner rate, and no crash rate is computed. Full audit, counters, and verification: verification.md.

## What this might mean

One reading is that the layer is two records in one: a dated audit program, in which conformance was measured and recorded wave by wave, and an undated register of locations with no ramp, most of it entered under ProjectType UNKNOWN. On that reading, the conformance figures describe the audited stock, and the No Ramp register is a separate work list whose size (961 locations) is the finding, not a failure rate. The strongest alternative explanation is that the No Ramp records are simply the tail of the same audit program - locations visited, found without a ramp, and recorded with the administrative fields left incomplete - in which case the full-denominator Yes share (49.5 percent) is the right headline basis and the audit-era decline partly records the program reaching harder locations in later waves. The layer alone cannot separate the two readings, because it does not document how the No Ramp records were collected; this brief therefore prints both bases (the full denominator and the determined-category denominator) and keeps No Ramp as its own category for that reason.

- **Nashville Department of Transportation and Multimodal Infrastructure (NDOT)** controls the curb-ramp inventory and the audit program it records. Concrete action: publish definitions for the ConformID categories and the DetectableWarning coding in the layer metadata, and document how the 961 No Ramp records were collected (dated audit or separate register), so the conformance shares can be read on a stated basis. Cost class: low-cost operational change (documentation within the existing program). Outcome measure: category definitions in the layer metadata at the service URL in sources.md, and the No Ramp count and the Unknown and Blank counts recomputed on a later snapshot of this layer.
